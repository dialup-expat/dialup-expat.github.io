I've spent just over 20 years as a professional software developer (now more of an agent director, unfortunately). It's always fun to reminisce about how it all started from me. Originally, I wanted to grow up to be Clark Kent. Not Superman -- my aspirations weren't that high -- but Clark Kent. An award winning, investigative journalist with some slick dark frames.

My desired career trajectory entirely changed sometime around 8th or 9th grade when a close friend -- who shall remain nameless lest the statute of limitations have not yet expired -- gave me a burned CD of "Visual Basic 6." I don't remember exactly why he gave it to me or where he himself acquired it. Maybe I expressed an interest or maybe I was over and he showed off dragging and dropping an application. Whatever the impetus, I was hooked.

Most developers or sysadmins my age can trace their roots back to something cool. Like programming for the Commodore 64 or an Apple II. I have VB6. I grew up looking over the fence at lower middle class, dreaming about one day. We didn't get our first computer until I was in 7th grade and, by then, it was some shitty hand-me-down Packard Bell i386. Don't get me wrong; I was ecstatic and grateful, but it would be another 1.5 years before dialup internet reached the household. The end of 8th grade was probably the first time I dipped my toe into the world wide web, and even then it was via AOL. As far as nerd credits go, I couldn't buy much.

But being able to drag a button on a window, give it a label, and have it do something onClick? Amazing. I cut my teeth scouring forums and tutorials, but learned most of what I knew from looking at other's source code. So what did I build with this new found pirated freedom? Why, tools to punt people off AOL/AIM, of course!

## Punters and Progz

My first foray onto the internet was via AOL, and I lived in chatrooms, mostly talking shit and being an asshole. I quickly learned -- via means I've now forgotten -- that there were certain sequences of characters you could copy and paste in instant message windows that would cause AOL or AIM to crash, effectively kicking the user offline. Why the hell was this so amusing? I don't know; I was an asshole.

My first apps (or programs, as we called them back then) were just that: tools to kick users off AOL or otherwise spam or try to look cool in chatrooms.

These tools were often called progz, proggies, or punters. I don't even remember what I called mine or how I distributed it. But it eventually got my ass and my whole family's asses kicked off AOL. I bet that was a fun conversation to have.

After that, we moved to CompuServe or some similar service for internet and I switched to using AIM (AOL Instant Messenger). That's when I had a bright idea that lead me to eventually steal 50-60 AIM screen names.

## An Early Phisher

I built an app that looked just like AIM's login screen. I spammed it around in various chat rooms advertising it as a new version of AIM, or a version of AIM with punters built right into it. When someone downloaded it and entered their username and password, it immediately sent it to me via some HTTP endpoint or email (I can't remember which) and then crashed. Then I would come along, change their password, and now forever own their screen name. The early 2000s were rife with poor security practices, and AIM did not require any sort of two factor auth or verification. Just enter your old password, enter your new password, confirm your new password, and congratulations on your stolen AIM username.

Those who were gullible enough to download it and enter their username and password were gullible enough to believe it simply crashed and moved on. I was sophisticated enough to have any sort of service ready to automatically change the password. I just woke up the next morning, saw my new catches, and went and changed all their passwords.

What was my endgame? This was the endgame. I had their screen names, and they didn't. Sometimes I would log into their accounts and argue with their older brothers who tried to get the name back. But mostly I just hoarded them on a list, content in knowing that I had them and they didn't.

I was an asshole. An asshole who amassed a few dozen screen names for no other reason than I could.

All because some buddy gave me a bootleg copy of VB6.

Eventually I learned how to program in other languages and made a career of it.

What an asshole.
