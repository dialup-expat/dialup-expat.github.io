In the very late 90s and early 00s, I became entranced with a website format called Everything/Nothing or E/N. It was essentially a bunch of websites that posted random blog articles with no clear connection between them. Think of an early livejournal or blog, mixed with a magazine? It's very hard to explain. Maybe [these guys](http://sawv.org/en.html) do a better job than I could. In addition to posts and articles, these sites also frequently had collections of random shit. MP3s, video game ROMs, wet tshirt contest photos, etc. All sorts of whatever felt right to the author or authors of that particular site.

These websites were frequently linked together via webrings, allowing you to discover near endless content of, well everything and nothing. There were also various index sites that used early hit tracking to track the most popular sites and list them. It was amazing. So of course my teenage self had to join in.

With the help of some friends from AIM/AOL chat rooms (I wasn't cool enough for IRC or BBS), I launched my own E/N website. I won't name it here; it was quite embarrassing and stupid and 100% still exists in the internet archive for me to feel ashamed about whenever I need to be knocked down a peg. I tried way too hard. I thought I was the teenage Howard Stern, but lacked the sense of humor or charm.

I'm proud that my own E/N website skyrocketed to the tops of these indexes. I'm embarrassed that it happened not on the merit of my content, but on me being a cheating asshole. Those same AIM chatrooms where I found my cowriters is where I would spam my site endlessly, often pulling a bate and switch, offering something else up but linking to my site instead. My [stolen army of screen names](@/blog/2026-11-13-of-programming-and-progz.md) kept me from getting banned and kept my spam new and fresh.

These early hit-count based tracking didn't account for bounce rates or time on site and, thus, up the charts I rose.

As blogs, LiveJournals, and Xangas took off, and eventually the rise of social networks commenced, E/N websites seemed to fade into even further obscurity. The charm -- and stank -- still live on in my nostalgic memory.
